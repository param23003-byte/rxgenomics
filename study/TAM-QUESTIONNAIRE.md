# TAM Acceptability Questionnaire — Draft

**Status: DRAFT for review by the Guide and the Ethics Committee.**
Not to be administered until approved. If the Ethics Committee approved a
specific wording, that wording replaces this draft.

Adapted from Davis FD. *Perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and user
acceptance of information technology.* MIS Q. 1989;13(3):319-340 (protocol
reference 26), with the referent changed from a generic system to RxGenomics
and to pharmacogenomic prescribing decisions.

## Administration

- 15 items, grouped into three constructs.
- 5-point Likert scale, as specified in the protocol:
  **1 = Strongly disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly agree.**
- Administered **after** the participant has completed their assigned case
  scenarios, together with the SUS.
- Present the items in a fixed order, but report construct scores, not item order.
- No item is reverse-scored, so a higher score always means greater acceptance.
  (This differs from the SUS, where alternate items are reverse-scored.)

## Scoring

Score each construct as the **mean of its items** (range 1–5), and report
mean ± SD per construct and per user group. Do not sum across constructs into a
single "TAM score": the three constructs are reported separately in the protocol's
endpoints (perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, intention to use).

Internal consistency is assessed with Cronbach's α per construct, with α ≥ 0.70
considered acceptable, after pilot testing on about 10 participants. Note that
the Intention to Use construct has only 3 items, so α is expected to be lower
there than for the 6-item constructs; report it with that caveat.

## Construct 1 — Perceived Usefulness (PU)

| # | Item |
|---|---|
| PU1 | Using RxGenomics would improve my performance in making genotype-guided prescribing decisions. |
| PU2 | Using RxGenomics would make it easier to apply pharmacogenomic guidelines in practice. |
| PU3 | Using RxGenomics would increase my productivity when reviewing a patient's medication. |
| PU4 | Using RxGenomics would help me identify patients at risk of an adverse drug reaction. |
| PU5 | Using RxGenomics would improve the safety of the prescribing decisions I contribute to. |
| PU6 | Overall, I would find RxGenomics useful in my clinical or academic work. |

## Construct 2 — Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU)

| # | Item |
|---|---|

| # | Item |
|---|---|

## Open-ended questions

Asked after the scaled items. Responses feed the thematic analysis alongside the
think-aloud transcripts.

1. What did you find most useful about RxGenomics?
2. What did you find most difficult or confusing?
3. What would need to change before you would be willing to use it in practice?
4. Is there anything you expected the system to do that it did not do?

## Points to settle before approval

1. **Clinician wording.** Items refer to "my practice or training" so that one
   form serves students, trainees and practising clinicians. If the Committee
   prefers separate forms per group, PU3 and ITU1–ITU2 are the items to reword.
2. **Item count.** 15 items plus the 10 SUS items is 25 scaled items per
   participant, which is reasonable after a 5–8 scenario session. If the
   Committee considers this too long, drop PU3 and PU5 (the two least specific
   usefulness items) to give a 13-item form.
3. **Anonymity.** The form carries only the participant ID (P-___) and group, in
   line with the protocol's anonymised questionnaire responses.
